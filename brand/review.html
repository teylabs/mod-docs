Packed square

A bento grid contained in a square, in warm tones. Every piece is a different size and fits exactly one way, like a real-world game of Tetris: irregular pieces packed perfectly, every one seated, with even gaps and flush edges. Tey orange marks one meaningful piece in each packing.

Three packings, each in two palettes: Clay (umber, clay and sand steps around the orange) and Ink (ink and paper tints with the orange as the only colour). The tones never carry the separation on their own; the gaps do, which is why mono and reversed still read. Each mark is generated at the size it’s shown, so every edge lands on a whole pixel at 16, 24 and 32 px.

Corners: the square’s four outer corners take the shared radius (1/24 of the size). Inner corners where pieces meet take half that, so each piece still reads as a separate physical block while the gaps stay even where four pieces meet; the inside corner of an L stays sharp so its gap keeps its width. At 16 px all of these round to nothing, which is fine.

round 4 · superseded before review, for reference

Complete

Every block seated, perfectly aligned, with even gaps, in a neat square. Nothing hovers, nothing is offset, and there are no ghost outlines. The orange block is simply the piece that completes the arrangement; its colour alone tells that story.

Each mark is generated at the size it’s shown, so every edge lands on a whole pixel at 16, 24 and 32 px. The gap is 1 px up to 31 px and 1/16 of the size above that, and the corner radius is 1/24 of the size. Where a grid can’t divide evenly at a small size, the spare pixels go to matching cells on both sides so the symmetry holds.

round 3 · for reference

The last piece

Keystone, refined into a neat square of modules with the orange piece as the last one going in. The square is already complete and tidy; the orange piece sits just above its gap or a hair out of place, about to drop in or click home. That’s the feeling the mark is after: irregular modules arranged into an organized whole.

Each mark is generated at the size it’s shown, so every edge lands on a whole pixel at 16, 24 and 32 px (the centre-joint blur from round 2 is gone). The gap is 1 px up to 31 px and 1/16 of the size above that, the corner radius is 1/24 of the size, and the orange is on the last piece only. From 48 px up a faint outline marks the gap it’s about to fill.

round 2 · for reference

Lift, in masonry

You picked Lift. Round 2 rebuilds it as masonry: blocks of different sizes, irregular but symmetrical, with the orange module lifted out. From 48 px up a faint ghost outline marks the slot it came from, so the mark says “copied”, not “moved”. Below 48 px the slot is simply empty.

Every variant uses the same rules: 4 to 6 blocks on a 48-unit grid, a 3-unit gap (1 px at 16), a 2-unit corner radius, and Tey orange #B65326 on the lifted block only. Round 1 is further down for reference. Wordmarks are live text for the review; the kit draws them as outlines.
